02Visitor creation

Tourist Design Studio

A creative stop where visitors turn something they loved about a trip into a T-shirt they help design. Choose a local theme, add a personal touch, and preview the keepsake.

Kids make the monsters. Grown-ups approve the shirt before it meets the credit card.

03Family play

Play through the journey

Family Journey

A shared adventure that gives children and grown-ups a reason to look closer. Follow a story, find a small clue, and make the destination part of the game.

Small explorers are welcome. Boring grown-up maps may feel slightly jealous.

Explore together Story-led quests Grown-ups involved
For
Children, parents and visitor venues
The moment
“Can the journey be part of the adventure?”
What the experience feels like

Pick a story before the visit, look for clues along the way, and complete a creative challenge together. Parents stay involved in the choices and decide what to keep or share afterward.

Craft & Collectibles

A way to discover and care for handmade pieces, artisan objects, and treasured finds. Bring the maker’s story, materials, and care notes together with the journey of the piece.

A little irregularity can be the nicest sign that a person made it.

Featured application

Every handmade piece has a story worth keeping.

A ceramic bowl, woven scarf, or carved keepsake carries something of the person who made it. Craft & Collectibles explores how that story, along with practical care and condition notes, can travel with the piece.

1

At the beginning

Meet the maker

Discover the workshop, materials, and techniques behind the piece, with the maker’s own notes and photos as a starting point.

→
2

On the way

Follow the piece

Keep a record of the piece as it moves from a workshop or shop to its new owner, including condition photos when they are useful.

→
3

At the end

Care for what you keep

Check the piece on arrival and keep its care instructions close: how to wash the textile, handle the glaze, or look after the wood.

From a small travel keepsake to a collectible artwork, the aim is the same: keep the maker’s story and the information that helps someone value and care for the piece.
